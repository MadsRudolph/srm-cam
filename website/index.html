SRM-CAM

KiCad in, a finished PCB out. The Roland SRM-20 mill drills the holes and cuts the board out; the xTool F1 Ultra fiber laser etches the copper, lined up on those holes. One app, one plan, two machines.

How a board is made

The usual way: the mill does the holes and the outline, the laser does the copper.

KiCadExportSet design rules for SRM-CAM for the laser, route, then Send to SRM-CAM.
MillDrill and cut outHoles and outline, on tabs. The board stays in its sheet: the laser marks the waste round it.
LaserCalibrateFinds the board by its holes, focuses, marks the waste to correct the camera, locks onto the holes.
LaserEtch the tracesCheck, re-etch spots. Then mask and silkscreen (still experimental) if you want them.
BenchBreak the tabsThe board is done.

Make a board walks through it at the machines.

Laser or mill alone?

The mill can still make a whole board by itself: drill, isolate the traces and cut out, in one run. It is slower and coarser than the laser, but it needs only the SRM-20.

Mill + laser (the usual way)Mill alone
Narrowest gap between nets0.20 mm0.8 mm with the flat bit, 0.44 mm with the ball mill
SpeedFaster: see the laser time belowSlower: the bit cuts every channel
Parts it suitsDense SMD too: gaps the bit cannot cutThrough-hole and larger SMD; SOIC and 0603 with the ball mill
MachinesSRM-20, then the xTool F1 UltraSRM-20 only
Mask, silkscreen, stencilOn the laser either way: mask and silkscreen lined up on the drilled holes, the stencil cut from steel foil

Laser time, the curtain-drive board: about 5 minutes with SRM-CAM's concentric rings, against about 2 hours for the same board through xTool Creative Space's own fill (the old way). That is laser against laser; no mill time is measured.

Use the mill alone when the laser is busy or not there, or for a simple through-hole board: Mill a whole board. Which way a board is made is its method, set in KiCad or picked from the chip at the top left of the window: see The method chip.
