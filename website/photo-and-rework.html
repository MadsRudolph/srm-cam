Photo check & rework

The traces pass finished — did every channel actually cut through? Overlay a photo of the board on the design to check, mark every failed spot with a box, and re-cut them all in one pass.

Rework basics

Everything lives on the Rework page. The board stays on the machine, same origin — never re-zero XY.

  1. Mark the spots Tick Add areas and drag a box over each spot that didn't cut. Every box gets its own color and a row in the table.
  2. Set depths New-box depth sets the default for the next box you draw; edit any box's depth individually in the table. Go slightly deeper than the original trace depth — the spot failed because the surface sat higher than the mesh believed.
  3. Keep boxes on the surface Each box's lvl toggle (default from Follow height map) warps that box to the probed surface so its depth stays uniform. Needs a probed or loaded height map.
  4. Export one file Export rework NC... writes a single <name>_<side>_<op>_rework.nc containing only the toolpaths clipped to your boxes, each at its own depth. Run it like any other job.

The photo overlay

Judging "did it cut?" by peering at the board in the machine is miserable. Instead, photograph the board and align the photo onto the design:

  1. Shoot the photo As straight-down and evenly lit as you can manage. Higher resolution is better — the detector needs it (see below). Avoid glare hot-spots on the copper.
  2. Load it Load photo... on the Rework page, then the anchor dialog opens: "Click the anchor holes in the photo."
  3. Click the four anchors The prompt names each anchor in order (e.g. bottom-left) with its machine coordinates. Click that hole in the photo. Navigation: scroll to zoom, drag to pan, right-click to undo the last anchor.
  4. Use the angle guide After the first click, a live rubber-band line follows your cursor showing the angle to the next anchor and its deviation from a perfect rectangle — green when square, orange when off, red when badly wrong. If the guide is red on a corner you're sure of, you're probably clicking the wrong hole.
  5. Blend photo and design Two sliders control the view: the photo opacity slider, and the Traces dim slider that fades the toolpaths so the photo underneath reads clearly at full opacity. Rework boxes stay full-strength regardless.

The photo is warped into machine coordinates, so a spot on the photo is that spot on the machine — boxes you draw over photo defects land exactly where the tool needs to go. The aligned photo is saved with the session.

Detect from photo

The Detect from photo button walks every isolation channel and inspects the aligned photo for stretches with no visible cut. Each suspicious region becomes a proposed rework box at the current New-box depth.

How it decides: a properly cut channel reads bright in a photo (exposed substrate floor and burred edges catch the light); a failed one reads flat and dark like the surrounding copper. The detector scores this cross-profile at full photo resolution, tile by tile. It was trained and validated against ground truth from a real production board — it found 20 of 21 operator-confirmed rework regions.

Know its limits
  • Too-shallow cuts are invisible. A channel cut at half depth still looks like a channel in any photo. The detector finds never-cut and badly failed stretches; judge depth failures with Probe boxes and Mesh check instead.
  • It refuses when it can't answer. Soft focus, glare, low resolution, or misaligned anchors make it decline rather than propose garbage. If it refuses, reshoot: closer, sharper, straighter.
  • Always review the boxes. Delete false alarms before exporting — you know the board.

Probe boxes: fix the depth, not just the spot

A rework spot exists because the real surface sat higher there than the height map believed. Probe boxes re-measures each box's center over SPI and compares against the leveling mesh: wherever the surface is higher than the mesh said, that box is automatically deepened by exactly the difference. Jog above the mesh reference point (leveling grid point 1) first, spindle OFF, red clip on the copper.

The complete rework loop

Photo → Detect from photo → review boxes → Probe boxes → Export rework NC... → run → re-photograph to confirm. Two or three minutes of checking instead of discovering a short after soldering.
